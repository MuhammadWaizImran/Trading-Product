## The model choice, in plain English

We are choosing a system with two different jobs, not training one giant model from scratch.

**Language and evidence:** Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 is the provisional explanation model. It is a pretrained 4-billion-parameter instruction model under Apache-2.0. Its job is to read retrieved evidence, extract supported facts and write a report with uncertainty and citations. Its size makes a cloud inference trial plausible within free resources, but actual memory, speed and account GPU availability must be measured. It has not yet been downloaded or evaluated for this project. General publisher benchmarks do not establish financial reliability. [Official card and license](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507).

Initially we give Qwen access to an attributed, rights-checked library rather than change its weights. This is retrieval: find the relevant passages, give them to the model with their sources, and require the answer to stay within that evidence. It lets us update or remove a source without retraining. Retrieval does not eliminate hallucinations, so citation support, arithmetic and missing-information behavior still need testing. Later small adapter fine-tuning is an option only if we can show a specific weakness and have original, reviewed examples to improve it. No full-model training is planned.

**Market prediction:** Logistic regression is the current reference model because it outperformed the small boosted-tree challenger on the two validation periods' probability error. It nevertheless failed the overall evaluation. It remains a benchmark, not an accepted production predictor. The next numerical experiment should compare a regularized return model against histogram gradient-boosted regression, predicting 24-hour return and downside outcomes. A regression model predicts a number; a classification model predicts a category/probability. This shift is motivated by the need to distinguish a likely tiny move from a move large enough to cover costs.

Trees can learn combinations such as momentum behaving differently when volatility is high. Linear models are simpler and easier to diagnose. Both fit structured market tables and can train on free cloud CPU. We will retain the simplest one that passes chronological tests; there is no final validated market model yet. More powerful hardware would not fix weak labels, inaccurate data or an unfair test.

**Research and decision rules:** Source lookup, arithmetic, freshness checks, risk limits and WAIT decisions should be explicit code around these models. They are not abilities to assume merely because an LLM sounds confident. No real-order tools are included.

## Rights and free-source boundaries

Ethereum.org generally licenses non-code content under CC BY 4.0, subject to marked exceptions; [terms](https://ethereum.org/terms-of-use/). EIPs have a [CC0 license](https://eips.ethereum.org/LICENSE). Mastering Bitcoin's repository states CC BY-SA 4.0; retain attribution and handle shared adaptations under the required terms. These are initial reusable knowledge candidates, not a corpus already ingested.

Coin Metrics is explicitly noncommercial. Other free APIs and public project pages may restrict copying, indexing, training or redistribution. Data with unresolved rights stays out of training. A third party labeling an exchange dataset CC0 does not establish that they owned all necessary rights. The existing SOURCE_REGISTER.csv distinguishes selected, reading-only and held sources.

## Preparation workflow

1. **Inventory and permission:** Define asset IDs, contract/chain, venue, dates, data frequency and legal use. Record missing coverage instead of silently fabricating it. Start with daily data; introduce hourly data only for a specifically justified horizon.
2. **Immutable raw snapshots:** Download in the cloud, save the original bytes and checksums, and record source URL, retrieval time and parser version. Keep raw data private.
3. **Clean and align:** Convert timestamps to UTC, normalize units, remove exact duplicate records only with an audit trail, quarantine conflicting duplicates, and flag gaps. Never forward-fill a halted asset into apparently tradable prices.
4. **Point-in-time join:** For every observation time T, include only facts whose publication/availability time is no later than T. Unknown historical availability means exclusion from the backtest. A record's event date alone is insufficient.
5. **Features and labels:** Compute past returns, trend, volatility, drawdown and volume changes from completed candles. Add fundamentals/news only after coverage and timing audits. Labels describe subsequent return and adverse outcomes after a realistic execution delay, net of declared costs. Fix horizon, costs and sample eligibility before training.
6. **Separate time periods:** Train on older periods, tune on later validation periods and lock a new test period. Purge overlapping outcome windows at boundaries. Fit normalization/imputation/calibration on training or dedicated calibration data only. The exposed 2024–2025 test cannot be reused as fresh confirmation.
7. **Build the reference library:** Keep knowledge text separate from price tables; retrieve passages with citations, dates and source status. Include contradictory evidence and unknowns. Treat external text as evidence, never operational instructions.
8. **Cloud evaluation and export:** Save versions, data hashes, models, predictions, calibration metrics, returns, costs, loss/drawdown, exposure and failure cases. Compare against cash, holding and realistic trend baselines. Also measure whether Qwen's statements are actually supported by cited evidence and whether it abstains when key information is missing.

Illustration: for an assessment dated 1 June, a supply announcement first published on 3 June is unavailable, even if it describes a 31 May event. Using it would teach the model from the future and inflate apparent performance.
