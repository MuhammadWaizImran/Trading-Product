LIQUIDITY: WHAT IS IT AND WHY IS IT IMPORTANT?





By definition, liquidity in the forex market refers to a currency pair’s capacity to be traded
(bought/sold) on demand. When you trade the biggest currency pairings, you do it on a
very liquid market. However, you trade using the financial institutions’ available liquidity,
which allows you to enter or exit the trade (currency pair) of your choice.
Not every pair of currencies is liquid. In actuality, the liquidity of currencies varies
depending on whether they are major, minor, or exotic pairs (including currencies from
emerging markets). As traders go from main pairs to minor pairs, then to exotic pairs, forex
liquidity decreases.


High Liquidity:
A currency pair with high liquidity in the forex market is one that may be purchased and
sold in sizable amounts without experiencing significant changes in its exchange rate (price
level), for example, major currency pairs like the EUR/USD.
Major currency pairs to be aware of include:
GBP/USD
USD/JPY
EUR/GBP
AUD/USD
USD/CAD
USD/CHF
NZD/USD

Low Liquidity
A currency pair with low liquidity in forex is one that cannot be bought or sold in sizable
amounts without causing major changes in its exchange rate price level, for example, exotic
currency pairs like the PLN/JPY.


Forex Liquidity vs. Illiquidity: 3 Major Signs to look out for
Because the volume of buying or selling at any given time might vary substantially, an
illiquid market will have chaotic fluctuations or gaps from the perspective of a trader. A
market with high liquidity is also described as being deep or smooth, and the price
movement is also smooth. Because it is exceedingly difficult to manage risk if you are on
the wrong side of a major move in an illiquid market, most traders need and should require
a liquid market.

with a big impact, such as one announcing interest rates, surprises everyone, there could be
price gaps in the FX market.
In the US, gaps can appear at the start of the week on Sunday afternoon. Overall forex gaps
are typically less than 0.50% of a currency’s value if a news event occurs over the weekend.

liquidity as a metric can accurately represent the retail market.

Different Times of the Day Offer Different Amount of Liquidity
Scalpers and short-term traders should be mindful of the fluctuations in forex liquidity
during the trading day. There are less active times, such as the Asian Session, which
frequently trades in a range, making support and resistance levels more likely to hold from
a speculative standpoint. Breakouts and bigger percentile changes over the day are more
common during the major moving market sessions, such as the US and London sessions.
Due to its overlap with the European/London Session, which alone accounts for around
+50% of daily worldwide volume, the US Morning Session is when you’re most likely to see
the greatest changes. Around 20% of trading takes place in the US session alone, and strong
moves typically decline sharply in the US afternoon save for the few occasions a year when
the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) makes a surprise statement.

Forex Market Hours and Liquidity
You can only execute trades with indices, stocks, and the majority of other financial
products that are traded on different international exchanges during those exchanges’ open
hours. Fortunately for forex traders, currencies are not subject to this restriction and can
be exchanged at any time of day or night because the forex market is open every day of the
week, 24 hours a day.


Taking advantage of trade opportunities at all times
Since the whole Forex market is operated electronically, inside a network of banks, it is
referred to as an Over-the-Counter (OTC) or “Interbank” market because it lacks a physical
presence or a central exchange. This implies that you can trade whenever it’s convenient
for you, 24 hours a day, using your broker.
Given that London is the primary hub for Forex trading, the 24-hour period shown below
depicts the times when the Interbank and Retail FX markets were open for business.
Nylon Session





Although trading activity is at its peak during the London session (as it is in Europe as
well), liquidity is at its peak when New York starts and the London session overlaps. The
time period between New York and London known as the “Nylon” session is when trade
volume and liquidity are at their maximum. It is therefore a good time to trade breakout
methods because we may anticipate larger swings. As a result of the higher volume and
liquidity, spreads are at their tightest during this time, which lowers transaction costs for
the traders.
Asia Session
While New York and London are regarded as significant trading hubs, Tokyo, Hong Kong,
and Singapore are also worthy of mention. Forex is viewed as a 24-hour market since Asian
banks open and trade as London and New York banks conclude their formal trading
sessions. However, because of the lower volume compared to the Nylon session, price
changes are typically smaller and spreads are greater.

price close/open in the case of a swing high and the range from price low to the minimum
between price close/open in the case of a swing low to determine how the swing area is
computed. The swing area for “Full Range” will be the entire candle range.
Use intra-bar data to compute the volume that has accumulated within a swing area for
more accurate results.
“Count” will filter out swing areas where price visited the area a lesser number of times
than the user-set threshold.

Usage


Utilizing the accumulation/distribution zones on lower timeframes, the liquidity swings
indicator can be utilized to identify big swing regions that could serve as future support or
resistance.





The indicator can only display significant swing areas with a higher level of liquidity by
filtering swing zones by volume.
These swing zones may be larger, indicating greater volatility, or they may have been
frequented more frequently by the price.
Keep in mind that swing points are verified after Pivot Look back bars, so all elements are
shown in reverse order.
